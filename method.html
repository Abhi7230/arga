How the data is collected

Preference data is only as good as the controls around it. This page states exactly how Arga's evaluation works, including the parts that limit what the data can tell you.

The task

Blind pairwise comparison. A rater sees two clips generated from the same prompt, side by side, and decides which is better on each craft criterion: A, B or Tie. Raters are never told which system produced which clip.

Default criteria: overall, visual quality, motion and physics, prompt alignment, lighting continuity, acting and performance. overall is always present and is the axis used for quality scoring and agreement. The criteria set is configurable per batch.

Bias controls

Position randomised
Left/right order is randomised per rater and per pair, recorded at assignment, and resolved back to the original A/B at export. Raters see "A" on the left and "B" on the right — those are screen positions, not model identities.
Opaque clip delivery
Clips are streamed through an anonymous endpoint. No model name or file name is visible in the interface or in the browser's network tab.
Randomised order
Each rater receives their queue in a different random order, so batch-position effects don't align across raters.

Quality controls

Two independent controls run on every batch. One measures whether a rater tracks ground truth; the other measures whether raters agree with each other.

Gold pairs
Items with a known correct answer, seeded invisibly into each rater's queue at roughly 10%. They are indistinguishable from normal items. Accuracy is scored on the overall choice, and raters below 75% are flagged for review.
Overlap set
Roughly 20% of non-gold pairs go to two raters, producing Cohen's κ per rater pair plus overall percent agreement.
Evidence requirement
Every rating carries a written reason of at least 25 characters that must cite a timestamp (e.g. 0:03). A judgement without a locatable moment is rejected at entry.
Confidence
Each rating carries a self-reported 1–3 confidence value, so low-certainty calls can be filtered or weighted.
Append-only
The ratings store refuses UPDATE and DELETE, and the application exposes no edit endpoint. A submitted rating is immutable.
Senior review
Overlap pairs where raters disagree on overall are surfaced with both reasons for review before delivery.

What you receive

ratings.csv
One row per rating, side mapping resolved to the original A/B, one column per criterion.
ratings.jsonl
The same records, one JSON object per line.
Report
Criteria breakdown with tie rates, per-rater gold accuracy, Cohen's κ per rater pair, the disagreement cases with reasoning, and a delivery-readiness table.
Methodology
This document, versioned with the batch.
manifest.json
Counts, SHA-256 checksums and generation time, so the pack can be verified.

Gold pairs are excluded from delivered exports — they measure raters, not models. Rater names never appear in any export, only stable pseudonymous rater IDs.

Stated limits

The things this data cannot tell you, said plainly rather than discovered later:

  • Time-on-task is browser-measured and can be gamed. Treat it as a signal, not a guarantee.
  • Percent agreement is not chance-corrected. Cohen's κ is reported alongside it for that reason.
  • Agreement and gold accuracy are computed on overall only, not per criterion.
  • Free-text reasons may say "left" or "right", which refer to screen position, not original A/B.
  • Rater pools carry their own aesthetic priors. A pool of Indian film professionals judges craft against the conventions they work in. That is the point of the data, and it is also a bound on it.
